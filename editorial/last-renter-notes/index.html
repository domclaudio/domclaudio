Written by someone who answered the Send Message button eleven hundred times last year.

The Concept

Apartments.com already has reviews. They're about buildings, they're unverified, and they skew toward the angriest month of someone's lease. None of that helps a renter deciding between unit 2420 and unit 2520 at the same price.

Last Renter Notes moves the review to the unit, verifies it from the lease, and gates it behind contribution. You can read one note anywhere for free. To read the rest, you leave one for your own place. The membership is the note.

Three rules make it defensible. Notes are anonymous but verified. After 48 hours nobody can edit them, including the writer. And the property gets the last word on every row, right above the button that sends them the lead. Renters get a witness. Properties that don't oversell get rewarded. Nobody's lead flow is touched.

Working title was Renter Squad. The copy renamed it.

Why it's built this way. The questions run generous to honest, so by the time someone reaches "no" they've already said something fair. "Why are you leaving?" is multiple choice on purpose. The essay is where bitterness lives; a single tap keeps it factual and lets it aggregate, so a unit that's turned over three times with "something about the apartment" tells its own story without anyone writing a bad review.

Column, not gate. Send Message is the lead, and the lead is what the property is paying for. Nothing stands between a renter and that button. This replaces the weakest column on the row instead, so the note sits at the exact moment of decision. The non-member state puts the give-to-get on every row of every listing. The empty state is a little sad on purpose. And the property response is what makes it sellable: they can't edit the note, but they get the last word on the row, right above the button.

The Launch

Email to renters who found their current place on Apartments.com
Apartments.comEmail · Launch
The next person in 4B has a question for you.
Two minutes. Then you can read everyone else's.

Hello Maya,

Sixteen months ago you found 4B on Apartments.com. Since then you've learned everything the listing couldn't tell you. Which burner runs hot. Whether the "quiet street" is quiet. What happens on garbage day.

Somebody's going to rent 4B after you. Right now they'll get the photos, the floor plan, and a tour. We'd like them to get you, too.

Introducing Last Renter Notes. A note from the last renter, on the unit, for the next one. Verified from your lease, posted anonymously, visible only when the unit lists again.

Here's the deal. You can read one note on any listing for free. To read the rest, you leave one for your place. That's the whole membership. No fee, no points, no badge. Just a note in a drawer that opens when someone needs it.

Leave a note for 4B

You don't have to be moving. You don't have to be unhappy. Most notes are things like "the rent was the rent" and "the light is real." The next renter would like to know that too.

Apartments.com

P.S. Your landlord can respond to your note. They can't edit it. Neither can we.

Seed the drawer. A give-to-get feature launches empty unless the people who can contribute on day one are asked on day one. This goes to renters whose current lease started on the site. It never says "review." Notes are for someone; reviews are about something.
