The routine that earns them

Eleven stages, first contact to renewal. No loose ends.

Reviews like those don't come from charm; they come from a pipeline that never sways regardless of who the prospect is — which is also the backbone of Fair Housing. Every lease I close runs through it.

  1. First contact

    Every lead gets a same-day response with availability matched to their need and a direct invitation to tour, with two concrete time options. Speed to lead is the cheapest conversion tool in the business.

  2. The tour

    Routed by the needs the prospect names. The anchor features never get skipped — the ones they forget to ask about are the ones they'd miss most as residents.

  3. Follow-up cadence

    Same-day thank-you with a recap, a value touch within 48 hours, then spaced touches until they lease here or lease elsewhere. Never "just checking in" — always a floor plan, a pricing update, an answer. All logged.

  4. Application onboarding

    One clear email the moment someone commits: the link, the document checklist, the fees, and what happens next. Receipt is timestamped, so pricing and priority order are never a dispute.

  5. Identity & income verification

    ID matched, income documented to standard, discrepancies resolved in writing before the file advances. I came up processing income certifications under bond-compliance audit standards; market-rate screening is the same discipline with fewer forms.

  6. Unit verification

    Once an applicant chooses a unit, I walk it. Discrepancies go to maintenance the same day, manager looped in. The unit gets its punch list weeks before move-in, not the morning of.

  7. Funds collection

    Every dollar receipted and timestamped the moment it's received. The file always answers three facts: who paid, how much, and when.

  8. File hygiene

    Nothing advances incomplete. One owner per file, version-controlled naming. A file I build can be pulled by a manager, an auditor, or an owner's rep six months later and read like a story with no missing pages.

  9. Move-in coordination

    On the calendar the moment the lease comes back signed. Utilities confirmed, insurance verified, keys staged, welcome sent. The first impression they get as a resident is the last step of my sale.

  10. Thirty days later

    A handwritten notecard at the resident's door. Thirty days in, the boxes are unpacked and the decision feels real — that's the moment to say thank you.

  11. Retention

    Lease-ups fill units; good consultants fill them with residents who renew. The second year is sold during week one — that's how a lease-up becomes a stabilized property.
