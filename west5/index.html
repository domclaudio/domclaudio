The Leasing Routine

Every lease I close runs through the same pipeline. From initial contact to handing keys to the newest resident of West 5th LA: the pattern is clear and made with intention.

Consistency is the backbone of Fair Housing: the order and standard never sway regardless of who the prospect is. Verification is the second layer; nothing is assumed, and every verification is recorded. The checklist ensures no loose ends.

01

Same day, every lead

First contact

Every lead gets a response the same day it arrives: an introduction to West 5th LA, the amenities that set it apart, availability matched to their need, and a direct invitation to tour with two concrete time options. Speed to lead is the cheapest conversion tool in the business. And every conversation closes the same way, booked or not: "I hope you find what you're looking for." Service for the prospect's needs supersedes the sale; that's the send-off they remember.

02

Built around the prospect

The tour

Reflects the initial interview: the needs and wants a prospect names shape the route we walk. Active listening and rapport carry the conversation, and the anchors never get skipped:

  • Private balcony
  • In-unit washer and dryer
  • Keyless entry
  • Rooftop decks with firepits
  • Resort-style pool
  • Coworking space and work-from-home pods
  • The resident speakeasy

The features a prospect forgets to ask about are the ones they'd miss most as a resident. And in City West, the neighborhood is an anchor of its own: a 93 transit score and a downtown commute measured in minutes sell themselves once they're said out loud.

03

Nothing goes cold by accident

Follow-up cadence

Follow-up runs on a set rhythm: a same-day thank you with a brief recap of what we covered on the tour, a value touch within 48 hours, then spaced touches until they lease here or lease elsewhere. A value touch is never "just checking in"; it's an attached floor plan, a pricing update, an answer to a question from the tour. All touches are logged.

04

No surprises mid-process

Application onboarding

The moment someone commits, they get one clear email: the application link, the document checklist, the fees, and what happens next. Application receipt is timestamped with a system capture saved to the file, so pricing and priority order are never a dispute.

05

Before anything gets signed

Identity and income verification

Government ID matched to the applicant; income documented to the property's standard and run through screening; discrepancies resolved in writing before the file advances. When two income figures conflict, the file shows which number was used and why.

I came up processing income certifications under bond compliance audit standards. Market-rate screening is the same discipline with fewer forms.

06

Walked before it's promised

Unit verification

Once an applicant chooses a unit, I walk it. Discrepancies and weaknesses go to the maintenance supervisor the same day, with the community manager looped in the entire time. The unit gets its punch list weeks before move-in, not the morning of.

07

Receipted and timestamped

Funds collection

Every dollar collected is receipted and timestamped the moment it's received: holding deposits, application fees, move-in funds. File updates always answer three facts: who paid, how much, and when.

08

No missing pages

File hygiene

Nothing advances without every component verified complete; a missing document holds the application until it's resolved. One owner per file, version-controlled naming. A file I build can be pulled by a manager, an auditor, or an owner's rep six months later and read like a story with no missing pages.

09

Confirmation, not a scramble

Move-in coordination

Move-in day goes on the calendar the moment the lease comes back signed. By then the unit has already been walked and punch-listed, and the repairs are already with maintenance; prep is confirmation. Utilities confirmed, renter's insurance verified, keys and remotes staged, welcome communication sent. The first impression they get as a resident, that this property is their home, is the last step of my sale.

10

Thank you, in ink

30 days later

A handwritten notecard left at the resident's door, thanking them for choosing West 5th LA. Thirty days in, the boxes are unpacked and the decision feels real; that's the moment to say thank you.

11

Sold during week one

Retention starts at first contact

Lease-ups fill units; good leasing consultants fill units with residents who renew. The follow-through after move-in, the 48-hour check-in, the fast response to the first work order, is what turns a lease-up building into a stabilized one. I sell the second year during the first week.
