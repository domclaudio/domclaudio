Problem
American Express reads as affluence: the lounge, the business traveler. When grocery bills climb, families on regular budgets assume Amex isn't for them, and another bank's card gets their everyday spending.
Insight
Amex isn't only for people who have money. It's for people who are good with it. Careful households already run a weekly grocery routine; they just don't see it as something that could pay them back.
Idea
Fifty-Two Trips. Groceries cost 2.2% more than they did a year ago. The Blue Cash Preferred gives 6% back at U.S. supermarkets. Tell it through one mom's year, and let December do the math.

I carry four Amex cards, and this is the one that bought my groceries back: $410 so far this year.

Why it's built this way. The voiceover is a question she gets asked every December and answers for us instead. She never says a percentage; the numbers live on the end card, where people expect fine print. The kid keeps Santa and the mother keeps the receipts — neither of them is wrong about who came. She isn't wealthy and she isn't struggling. She pays the balance in full every month. Carrying one would eat the rewards before she saw them.
